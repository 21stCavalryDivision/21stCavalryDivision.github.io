SOP 1-01

General Unit Standards

Members of the 21st Cavalry Division are expected to maintain professionalism, follow the established chain of command, communicate clearly, and contribute to a positive and organized unit environment.

During official training and operations, personnel will remain focused on their assigned responsibilities and follow instructions issued by designated leadership. Members are expected to support the mission, their team, and the overall effectiveness of the unit.

Core Expectations

  • Treat members, guests, and staff with respect.
  • Arrive prepared for scheduled training and operations.
  • Follow the established chain of command.
  • Perform assigned roles to the best of your ability.
  • Follow event-specific instructions and briefings.
  • Participate constructively in training and after-action reviews.
  • Maintain a cooperative team-oriented attitude.
  • Avoid conduct that intentionally disrupts official unit activities.

Professional Conduct

Members are expected to maintain a reasonable level of professionalism within official unit spaces. Harassment, deliberate disruption, excessive toxicity, repeated disrespect, or behavior that damages the unit environment may result in administrative action.

SOP 2-01

Communications

Unit communications should remain brief, clear, and relevant. Operational channels are kept free of unnecessary traffic so leaders and teams can pass important information efficiently.

Radio Discipline

  • Listen before transmitting.
  • Identify the intended receiving station or callsign.
  • Keep transmissions concise and relevant.
  • Avoid unnecessary conversation on operational frequencies.
  • Acknowledge instructions when appropriate.
  • Repeat important information when clarification is required.
  • Move lengthy conversations to another channel when possible.

Reporting

Reports should contain the information needed by leadership without unnecessary detail. Members should provide accurate location, situation, personnel, equipment, and mission information when applicable.

Callsigns

Assigned callsigns should be used during official operations. Personnel should avoid replacing assigned callsigns with names, nicknames, or unrelated identifiers when communicating over operational radio channels.

TRAINING & DOCTRINE

TRADOC

TRADOC policies establish the training, qualification, evaluation, and progression standards used throughout the unit.

TRADOC 1-01

Training & Qualifications

All new members complete initial orientation and basic training before progressing into advanced or specialized qualifications.

Specialized billets may require additional instruction, evaluation, or qualification by an approved instructor before a member may perform the role during official operations.

Training Expectations

  • Members should arrive on time and prepared for instruction.
  • Members should follow instructor directions during training.
  • Questions are encouraged when clarification is needed.
  • Members should demonstrate the required skill before qualification.
  • Instructors should maintain consistent training standards.

TRADOC 1-02

Basic Training

Basic Training provides new members with the foundational skills needed to participate safely and effectively in unit activities.

Training Subjects

  • Unit organization and chain of command.
  • Basic communications and radio procedures.
  • Individual equipment and loadout standards.
  • Movement and formations.
  • Basic navigation.
  • Medical fundamentals.
  • Weapons handling and range procedures.
  • Basic team-level tactical procedures.
  • Mission preparation and briefing procedures.
Training Completion Successful completion of Basic Training is required before a new member is considered fully qualified for normal operational assignment.

TRADOC 1-03

Specialized Qualifications

Specialized positions require additional knowledge and responsibility. Personnel may be required to complete qualification training before serving in advanced billets.

Specialized Areas

  • Aviation.
  • Armor and vehicle crews.
  • Reconnaissance.
  • Medical roles.
  • Crew-served weapons.
  • Forward observation and fire support.
  • Leadership positions.
  • Instructor positions.

TRADOC 1-04

Training Standards

Training events should provide practical instruction, repetition, and evaluation relevant to a member's assigned duties.

Instructor Responsibilities

  • Prepare training material before the scheduled event.
  • Explain the purpose and objective of the training.
  • Demonstrate required procedures when appropriate.
  • Allow members reasonable opportunities to practice.
  • Correct mistakes professionally.
  • Evaluate members using consistent standards.
  • Record qualifications when required.

TRADOC 1-05

Remedial & Refresher Training

Remedial or refresher training may be provided when a member requires additional instruction or when a qualification has not been used for an extended period.

The purpose of remedial training is to improve performance and reinforce unit standards rather than to punish members for reasonable mistakes.

ADMINISTRATION

ADMIN

Administrative policies establish standards for attendance, leave, accountability, communication, and membership status.

ADMIN 1-01

Attendance

Members should make a reasonable effort to attend scheduled unit events and communicate when real-life obligations prevent participation.

Active members should attend at least two official unit events per month unless excused by their chain of command.

Consistent unexplained inactivity may result in reassignment to reserve status so active billets remain available for participating members.

Attendance Expectations

  • Monitor unit announcements and schedules.
  • Notify leadership when unable to attend a required event.
  • Avoid repeated no-call/no-show absences.
  • Maintain reasonable communication with the unit.
  • Request leave when expecting extended inactivity.
Real Life Comes First Employment, family obligations, education, emergencies, and other legitimate real-life responsibilities take priority. Members are expected to communicate when possible.

ADMIN 1-02

Leave of Absence

Members who expect to be unavailable for an extended period should notify their chain of command and request a Leave of Absence when appropriate.

Approved leave allows leadership to plan around the member's absence while allowing the member to maintain standing within the unit.

LOA Expectations

  • Notify leadership as early as reasonably possible.
  • Provide an estimated return date when known.
  • Sensitive personal information is not required.
  • Notify leadership if the expected return date changes.
  • Contact the chain of command when ready to return to active status.

ADMIN 1-03

Chain of Command

Members should use the established chain of command for questions, requests, concerns, and unit-related matters.

Issues should normally be handled at the lowest appropriate leadership level before being elevated to higher command.

Escalation

  • Begin with your immediate team or squad leadership.
  • Elevate unresolved matters through the next leadership level.
  • Serious or sensitive concerns may be taken directly to appropriate command staff.
  • Emergency matters do not require unnecessary delays through normal channels.

ADMIN 1-04

Professional Conduct

Members should maintain a respectful environment in unit Discord channels, servers, training events, operations, and other official community spaces.

Unacceptable Conduct

  • Harassment of other members.
  • Deliberate disruption of training or operations.
  • Repeated disrespect toward members or leadership.
  • Intentional misuse of administrative permissions.
  • Actions intended to damage the unit or its community.

ADMIN 1-05

Inactivity

Members who become inactive without communication may be contacted by leadership to determine their status.

Extended inactivity may result in reassignment to reserve status or removal from an active billet.

Members returning after extended inactivity may be required to complete refresher training before resuming certain specialized responsibilities.

OPERATIONS

Operational Standards

Operational policies establish expectations for preparation, equipment, organization, and conduct during official missions.

OPS 2-01

Operational Readiness

Personnel should arrive before scheduled operations with required mods updated, communications working, and assigned equipment prepared.

Pre-Operation Requirements

  • Review operation announcements.
  • Update required server mods.
  • Verify microphone and communications.
  • Know your assigned billet.
  • Review available briefing information.
  • Prepare required equipment before formation.

OPS 2-02

Equipment & Loadouts

Members will use equipment appropriate to their assigned role and follow established unit loadout standards during official operations.

Equipment Standards

  • Use equipment appropriate to the assigned billet.
  • Avoid unnecessary equipment that does not support the mission.
  • Specialized weapons should be used by assigned personnel.
  • Crew-served equipment should be operated by trained personnel.
  • Aviation equipment should be used by assigned aviation personnel.
  • Vehicle crews should use equipment appropriate to their assigned platform.

OPS 2-03

Formation & Briefing

Members should report to their assigned element before the operation briefing and remain available for accountability.

Personnel should listen to the briefing, understand the mission, identify their role, and ask necessary questions before the operation begins.

OPS 2-04

After-Action Review

After-action reviews are used to identify strengths, problems, lessons learned, and opportunities for future improvement.

AAR Standards

  • Keep feedback constructive.
  • Discuss actions and procedures rather than attacking individuals.
  • Identify what worked well.
  • Identify areas requiring improvement.
  • Recommend practical solutions when possible.

PERSONNEL

Personnel Management

Personnel policies establish standards for promotion, assignments, billets, activity status, and professional development.

PERSONNEL 3-01

Ranks & Promotions

Promotions are based on demonstrated leadership, reliability, activity, training completion, unit contribution, performance, and available positions.

Time in the unit alone does not guarantee promotion.

Promotion Considerations

  • Attendance and activity.
  • Training completion.
  • Performance in assigned duties.
  • Leadership ability.
  • Professional conduct.
  • Reliability.
  • Unit requirements.
  • Availability of appropriate billets.

PERSONNEL 3-02

Billets & Assignments

Billets identify a member's primary responsibility within the unit. Assignments are based on qualifications, performance, activity, unit requirements, and available positions.

Command may reassign personnel when necessary to maintain effective unit organization.

PERSONNEL 3-03

Reserve Status

Reserve status is available for members who cannot consistently meet active attendance requirements but wish to remain affiliated with the unit.

Reserve personnel may return to active status when their availability improves and an appropriate billet becomes available.

PERSONNEL 3-04

Qualification Records

Leadership and instructors may maintain records of member qualifications, training completion, and specialized certifications when required for unit organization.

Members should notify leadership if a qualification record appears inaccurate or incomplete.

COMMAND

Leadership Standards

Command policies define the responsibilities, expectations, and authority of unit leadership.

COMMAND 4-01

Leadership Responsibilities

Unit leaders are responsible for maintaining accountability, communicating expectations, developing personnel, conducting training, and supporting organized operations.

Leadership Expectations

  • Lead by example.
  • Maintain reasonable accountability of assigned personnel.
  • Communicate schedules and expectations.
  • Support member development.
  • Address problems professionally.
  • Maintain consistent standards.
  • Support the command structure and mission.

COMMAND 4-02

Orders & Authority

During official operations and training, members are expected to follow reasonable instructions issued by designated unit leadership.

Leaders should exercise authority professionally and keep instructions consistent with unit policies, training objectives, and mission requirements.

COMMAND 4-03

Personnel Accountability

Leaders should maintain awareness of the status, assignment, and availability of personnel under their responsibility.

Accountability Includes

  • Personnel assigned.
  • Personnel present.
  • Personnel absent or excused.
  • Training and qualification status.
  • Current billet assignment.
  • Reserve or leave status when applicable.

COMMAND 4-04

Leadership Development

Members being considered for leadership positions should demonstrate reliability, communication ability, sound judgment, knowledge of unit standards, and the ability to work effectively with others.

Prospective leaders may be given opportunities to assist with training, planning, accountability, or temporary leadership responsibilities before permanent assignment.

UNIT REFERENCE

Policy Updates

Unit policies may be updated as training standards, organizational requirements, or operational needs change. Members are responsible for reviewing current published standards and official unit announcements.

Questions About Policy Members who need clarification regarding any SOP, administrative policy, qualification requirement, or unit standard should contact their chain of command.
